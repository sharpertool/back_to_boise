How the lines were drawn

Ed’s three corridors are the middle (I-15 through Salt Lake and St. George), the east side of the Wasatch (Provo and Price), and the west (US-93 through Ely). The map draws four routes plus a dashed reference. Miles below are a car router (OSRM), not a tow. Google Directions on the map replaces the line when that service answers. An earlier OSRM set was about 1,067 miles for the Las Vegas route, about 1,059 for a shorter Salt Lake–Flagstaff–I-17 line, and about 932 for US-93 via Twin Falls and Ely.

  • A · Las Vegas (middle). I-84 to Salt Lake, I-15 through St. George and Las Vegas, US-93 via Kingman and Wickenburg to Phoenix. This trace: about 1,065 miles, 19.3 hours by car.
  • B · Grand Staircase / Page. I-84 to Salt Lake, I-15 to St. George, then UT-59 and AZ-389 through Colorado City and Fredonia to Kanab, US-89 via Page to Flagstaff, I-17 to Phoenix. The Zion–Mt. Carmel Tunnel is not on this line. A combined vehicle is limited to 50 feet overall, the trailer to 26 feet from hitch to rear axle, 7 feet 10 inches wide, and 11 feet 4 inches tall (NPS). This trace: about 1,092 miles. It is longer than the 1,059-mile Salt Lake–Flagstaff figure because it drops to St. George first.
  • C · Great Basin. Modeled as I-84 to Twin Falls, US-93 via Jackpot and Wells to Ely, US-50/US-6 east to Delta, then I-15 south through Fillmore, St. George, and Las Vegas, then US-93 via Kingman and Wickenburg. This is the Jackpot option, not “out to Snowville, then I-80 west back to Wells.” This trace: about 1,146 miles, 22.4 hours by car.
  • D · East of the Wasatch. I-84 to Salt Lake, I-15 to Spanish Fork, US-6 through Price to Green River, I-70 west to Salina, US-89 south through Panguitch and Kanab (not UT-12, and not the Zion tunnel), US-89 to Page and Flagstaff, I-17 to Phoenix. Capitol Reef on UT-24 and Richfield (about 20 miles west of Salina on I-70) are side trips, not the through-line. This trace: about 1,163 miles.
  • Reference · US-93 direct. Same as C until Ely, then US-93 south all the way to Las Vegas, Kingman, Wickenburg, and Phoenix. No Delta jog. This trace: about 919 miles, 18.4 hours by car. The earlier 932-mile figure is the same corridor measured a different way.

Boise to Elko on that US-93 path (I-84, US-93, I-80) is about 300 miles and a little over 5 hours by car, not 400. Elko to Ely via Wells is about 189 miles and 3.6 hours by car. Both are weekend mornings, not 3 p.m. starts. November and December days on this run are short, on the order of 9.5–10 hours. Be parked while you can still see the site.

Read before you go

  • Cold sits. Elko is about 5,000 feet and Ely about 6,400 feet. November and December nights there run to the teens and single digits. US-93 and US-50 can take snow. The X195 is Imperial-rated to about −40°F with the heat and heated tanks on. Freeze risk remains if the heat is off. Page, Kanab, and Flagstaff are high enough to snow. St. George and Phoenix are the mild end.
  • Water. Many spigots shut off after October. The note on each park says what was actually published. Call before you count on a hose. Do not leave a hose connected overnight when it will freeze.
  • Nevada BLM. Unless a site posts otherwise, dispersed camping is 14 days in any 28-day period, then move at least 25 miles. BLM Nevada.
  • St. George BLM. Dispersed camping is not allowed inside Red Cliffs National Conservation Area, within a mile of Red Cliffs Recreation Area or Baker Dam Recreation Area, or within a half mile of the Smithsonian Butte Backcountry Byway. Stay limit in that field office is 14 days inside a 30-mile radius in 28 days. Field office camping brochure.
  • Pins are search areas. Each dot is a named park, campground, or byway center from the agency page, Recreation.gov, or the USGS names layer. It is the place you drop into onX. It is not a secret dispersed pad.
